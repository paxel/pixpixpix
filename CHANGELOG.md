# Changelog

All notable changes to this project will be documented in this file.

The format is based on [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/).

## [0.1.0]

### Added

- `pixpixpix <file>` (or the short `pix3`) shows the image as fast as possible; a folder or no argument shows the folder's first image. JPEG, PNG, GIF, WebP, BMP and TIFF, upright according to EXIF orientation; HEIC/HEIF and AVIF too through libheif, loaded on the first such file (a missing libheif or decoder shows as a broken file with the reason). `Enter` refuses to write a turn into HEIC/HEIF and AVIF.
- Animated GIF and WebP show their first frame at once, then play and loop once all frames are decoded (delays under 20 ms play as 100 ms, as in browsers; an animation over 512 MB of pixels stays still); their band thumbs carry an animated badge.
- Multi-page TIFF, MPO and multi-image HEIC/HEIF show which of their images is shown ("2 / 5") and their band thumbs carry the image count; Up/Down in the image view step through the images, starting at the first again on each visit. Reduced-resolution TIFF pages, MPO thumbnails and Ultra HDR gain maps do not count. `Enter` refuses to write a turn into such files, as the other images would be lost.
- Videos (mp4, mov, m4v, mkv, webm, avi, mts, m2ts, 3gp, wmv, flv, mpg) are part of navigation through libmpv, loaded on the first video: the main view and the band show the first frame with a play triangle; `Space` or a click plays and pauses, with sound at system volume, and `m` mutes. Left/Right still change the file and stop playback; at the end the first frame shows again. A video that cannot play shows mpv's error (or that libmpv is missing) with the `o` hint, over its first frame when there is one. Frames are drawn in software, at most 1920 pixels a side.
- Video seeking: `Shift+Left/Right` seeks 10 s, `Ctrl+Left/Right` (`Cmd` on macOS) 30 s and `Alt+Left/Right` steps one frame and pauses; a video not playing shows step buttons (-30s, -10s, -1 frame, +1 frame, +10s, +30s). Only one seek runs at a time, further steps move its target, so rapid or held stepping always ends on a drawn frame.
- Apple Live Photos (a HEIC/JPEG and a MOV of the same name) are one entry, the MOV hidden from navigation and the band, and `x` trashes both, naming each file that could not be trashed. Google and Samsung Motion Photos (JPEGs with the video inside) are detected once the first image is shown. Both carry a LIVE/MOTION badge in the band and a "Live Photo"/"Motion Photo" mark in the main view; `Space` plays their video over the photo, with pause, mute and seeking as for videos, and the photo shows again at the end. The video of a Motion Photo plays from a copy in the cache directory, removed when it stops.
- Left/Right arrows step to the previous/next image of the folder in natural name order, stopping at the ends with a "first image"/"last image" hint; the bottom key line lists the available keys.
- Up to 5 neighbours per side of the current image are preloaded after it is shown, so stepping to them is instant; a thin band above the key line shows them as thumbnails, marks the current one and jumps to a clicked one. Loading that is no longer needed is cancelled mid-read.
- A file that fails to decode shows the decoder's error and the hint that `o` opens it in another app; its band thumb carries an error marker and navigation still stops on it. A folder that cannot be read is reported in the key line.
- The folder is watched once the image is shown: files other programs add, remove or rename show up in navigation and the band on their own; when the shown file is removed, the next one is shown.
- Images fit the window by default; `+`/`-` and the mouse wheel zoom (the wheel toward the pointer), dragging pans, `0` fits and `1` shows 100 %. Zoom level and position carry over to the next image.
- `n` opens a column listing the subfolders of the current folder: Up/Down pick one, Right/Enter enter it, Left/Esc/Backspace go to the parent. A new folder opens like a start there, its first image first, and all loading for the old folder is cancelled. The key line lists the keys of the focused area.
- While the dir column has the focus, the main view and the band preview the first image of the highlighted subfolder; a subfolder without images shows "No images" with its subfolder count. The first images of the parent and the highlighted subfolder are preloaded, so going up or entering shows an image at once. A folder without images given at start gets a dir column too, and a folder that cannot be read shows why.
- In the dir column, `.` shows dot files and dot folders and `r` searches the subfolders breadth-first for the nearest folder with images, filling the band with them; picking one moves there. Both last until the column leaves the folder; with `Shift` they stay on in every folder entered. The key line tags what is on.
- `g` opens an overview of the current folder, or of the folder highlighted in the dir column, and all folders below it, in place of the image view and the band: a scrollable section per folder with images, headed by its path relative to the root (the root by its name), in natural depth-first order with a folder's own files first. Names and placeholders show at once while the scan runs in the background; thumbnails are decoded four at a time and only for the tiles on screen, videos by their first frame with a play triangle (a placeholder without libmpv), Live Photos with their badge. Thumbnails are read from and written to the freedesktop thumbnail cache (`~/.cache/thumbnails`, on macOS `~/Library/Caches/pixpixpix/thumbnails`), so a second visit decodes nothing; outdated ones are made again. Dot folders are skipped unless `.` is active, empty and symlinked folders are left out. `g` closes it again, back to the image view as it was.
- In the overview the arrows move a selection over the thumbnails, row by row across sections; `Enter` or a click shows the file in the image view in its folder, and `g` brings the overview back at the same spot without scanning again. `x`, `c` and `o` trash (a Live Photo with its MOV), copy and open the selected file elsewhere. `+`/`-` and `Ctrl`+wheel (`Cmd` on macOS) step the thumbnail size from 96 to 320 points, remembered in `settings.toml`. `Tab` moves between gallery and dir column: there Up/Down scroll to the highlighted folder's section, Right/Enter and going up make the column's folder the new root and abort the running scan.
- `b` focuses the band: Left/Right move its highlight, Enter shows the highlighted image (a found one too) and returns to the image view, Esc goes back. `Tab` cycles dir column, image view and band, skipping the closed column.
- `s` opens the settings screen (`s` or `Esc` closes it): the number of images preloaded per side (1 to 20, default 5) takes effect at once and is saved to `settings.toml` in the config directory, which may also be edited by hand; a file that cannot be read keeps the defaults and the screen shows why.
- `o` opens the current file, a broken one too, in the system default app (`xdg-open` on Linux, `open` on macOS) and pixpixpix stays open; the settings screen can name one app for images and one for videos instead (`image_app`/`video_app` in `settings.toml`). A failure to launch is shown in the key line with its message.
- `l`/`r` turn the shown image a quarter left/right on screen only; `Enter` writes the turn into the file, JPEG losslessly (needs libturbojpeg) and other formats re-encoded, with the EXIF orientation baked in. Moving to another image without `Enter` drops the turn and leaves the file untouched; a failed write is shown in the key line.
- `x` moves the current file, a broken one too, to the system trash and shows the next image at once, or the previous one after the last; a folder left without images says so and opens the dir column. A failure brings the file back and is shown in the key line.
- `c` puts the shown image, with a pending turn, and the file itself on the clipboard at once, so pasting into an image app gives the picture and pasting into a file manager copies the file; the key line says `copied` or why not. Linux only for now, through X11 (on Wayland via XWayland).
- `p` shows a toolbar above the image: drag on the image to draw arrows in one of six colours and three sizes (relative to the image), `Ctrl+Z`/`Ctrl+Shift+Z` (`Cmd` on macOS) or its buttons undo and redo, and a Settings button opens the settings screen. `Enter`, leaving the image or quitting with unsaved arrows opens the save dialog: Overwrite (not offered for HEIC/AVIF, multi-image files and Motion Photos), Save as new with an editable name (`<name>_edit.<ext>`, then `_edit2`…, PNG for HEIC/AVIF; never replacing a file), Copy to clipboard, or Undo edits; `Enter` saves as new, `Esc` goes back to editing, and the interrupted move or quit goes on afterwards. The saved file shows the arrows and a pending turn as on screen, without the original's metadata.
- The toolbar also draws text (click, type, `Enter` or a click elsewhere ends it), rectangle and ellipse outlines, freehand pen lines, a see-through highlighter box and pixelated areas whose content the saved file really hides, and crops freely: the Crop tool frames the part kept with drag handles, any other tool shows only that part. Each mark or crop is one undo step and the saved or copied image matches the screen.
- The toolbar's Export tool writes an area of the edited image at exactly the pixel size entered (remembered in `settings.toml`): the area is drawn and adjusted with corner handles in the ratio of that size, or with "Lock ratio" off drawn freely and stretched to it. JPEG, PNG or WebP (lossless, without a size limit); for JPEG and PNG an optional maximum size in KB lowers the JPEG quality or raises the PNG compression until the file fits, else says why. Exports carry no metadata. The save dialog offers a new file named `<name>_<W>x<H>.<ext>` or the clipboard (the pixels only); it never overwrites the original.
- The tools and the export also work on a paused video frame (`p` pauses a playing video): the result is an image, the save dialog offers no Overwrite and names the new file `<video name>_<HH-MM-SS.mmm>.png` after the frame's position. Playing, seeking or leaving the video with unsaved edits asks first.
- `i` shows a metadata panel beside the image: file name, size, modification time and image size, and from the EXIF of JPEG, PNG, WebP, TIFF and HEIC the camera, lens, capture date and time zone, exposure, ISO, focal length, GPS position and orientation. The capture date is the one editable field: `Enter` checks it (`YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM:SS`) and writes it in place into the existing date tags of JPEG, PNG, WebP and TIFF without touching the pixels; a file without a capture date has none to edit.
- The metadata panel's "Remove location" and "Remove all EXIF" buttons go through the save dialog: Overwrite (not offered for HEIC/AVIF, multi-image files and Motion Photos), Save as new (`<name>_noloc.<ext>` / `<name>_noexif.<ext>`, same format), or Copy to clipboard (the pixels only). JPEG, PNG and WebP: removing the location clears the GPS tags where they stand and keeps all other metadata and the pixels; removing all EXIF drops the EXIF block, after turning a non-upright image upright (JPEG losslessly through libturbojpeg, refused without it; PNG and WebP re-encoded). XMP that names a place goes too. HEIC/AVIF, multi-image files, Motion Photos and other formats are refused with the reason.
- The window reopens at its last size and position; `f` toggles fullscreen; `Ctrl+Q` (`Cmd+Q` on macOS) quits.
- `PIXPIXPIX_TIMING=1` prints the time from launch to the first frame.
- On Linux, `brew install paxel/tap/pixpixpix` installs `pixpixpix` and `pix3`. After its first frame pixpixpix puts its icon and launcher entry into `~/.local/share` (only replacing an entry it wrote itself), so it shows in the application menu with its icon, also on Wayland, and in "Open with" for every supported image and video type, without ever becoming the default app.
- On macOS (Apple Silicon), `brew install --cask paxel/tap/pixpixpix` installs `pixpixpix.app` with libmpv inside, plus the `pixpixpix` and `pix3` commands; the app is unsigned (see the install caveats) and Finder lists it in "Open with" for every supported image and video type, never as the default app (picking it there starts pixpixpix, but does not open the chosen file yet).
- On Linux, a Flatpak bundle for x86_64 and aarch64 on each release, installed for the current user with `curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/paxel/pixpixpix/summer/packaging/flatpak/install.sh | sh`, which also fetches the runtime from Flathub and adds the `pixpixpix` and `pix3` commands to `~/.local/bin`. The Flatpak sees the home, `/media`, `/run/media` and `/mnt`.
